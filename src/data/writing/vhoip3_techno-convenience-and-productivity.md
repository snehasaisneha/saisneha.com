Technology is often multiple not a single entity. It is, in reality, distinct concepts colliding and integrating in ways the scientists - or technologists - often don't anticipate. I don't just mean the collision of mathematics and physics and chemistry and applied research that was requisite in creating the modern smart phone, for e.g..

One part of technology is the scientific advanced in the technology itself. A petrol car with rubber tyres is more *scientifically advanced* in comparison to a horse cart because it represents the ingenuity in combining various scientific innovations into one. There was innovation taking the *primitive* wheel and using rubber to make it run smoother and faster. There was innovation in figuring out how to create rubber from trees. There was innovation in figuring out how oil can be used to run the engine of cars, and innovation in designing the engine and the gearbox to actually start the car and then maintain and increase speed. There was also innovation in how to manufacture the car faster using an assembly line. Finally, there was innovation in building roads that make it smooth for cars to drive on. I have used the word *innovation* above without assigning it any ethical connotations. But I do believe there are ethical connotations to every step of the process. But more on that later.
